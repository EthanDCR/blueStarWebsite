The map results are the prize

Three businesses get these spots, and most people never scroll past them. They're chosen mainly by your Google Business Profile — how close you are to the searcher, how many reviews you have, and what categories you picked. Not by your website.

The website ranks below that

It's still worth building — it catches people who scroll, it's where customers decide whether to call you, and it feeds Google information that helps the map listing too.

So the order of work is

Google Business Profile and reviews first, website second. The website is already underway; the profile needs you.

Group one

Needed before the site can go live

These are facts only you have. Nothing else can be finalised without them.

Your business name, spelled exactly as you'll file it Blue Star Welding Co. — or Company? Period after Co?

Google checks your name against everywhere it appears online. If the site says one thing and Facebook says another, Google isn't confident they're the same business — and you rank lower for it. We pick one spelling and use it everywhere.

Business phone number Ideally a 940 number

This shows up directly in search results, so it has to be a number you answer. A local area code is a small free signal that you're actually in Denton.

Who answers that phone, and when And whether you'll take texts

More jobs are lost to unanswered phones than to bad websites. If you're under a hood most of the day we should plan for it — a voicemail you return same-day, or putting texting front and centre, which is how most people want to reach a tradesman now.

A street address Google can verify Your home address is fine

Google ranks you partly on how close you are to whoever is searching, so your address decides how far out you show up. It stays private — we hide it and show a service area instead — but Google has to confirm it's real, usually by mailing a postcard.

Do you carry general liability insurance? If yes, what's the coverage limit?

Ranch and commercial customers often ask for proof before you set foot on the property. And we can only put "insured" on the site if you actually are — so we need a yes or no, not a guess.

Any welding certifications? AWS, pipe, or none

Having none is completely fine for repair and fence work — we just build the site around what's true. What we can't do is claim a certification you don't hold; that's the kind of thing a customer checks when something goes wrong.

A domain name bluestarweldingco.com, or similar

Needs buying before launch, and it should be registered in your name. It also gives you an email on your own domain, which reads a lot more established than a Gmail address on a quote.

Group two

Needed before you'll show up in searches

The site can launch without these. It just won't rank without them.

Check whether a Google listing already exists for you Search your name on Google Maps before we make one

Google auto-creates listings from scraped data all the time, and people often have one without knowing. Two listings compete with each other and are a headache to merge later, so we find out first.

Set up and verify the Google Business Profile The single biggest item on this page

This is what puts you in the map results. It's free, it takes about thirty minutes, and verification takes a week or two for the postcard to arrive. Everything else is supporting cast.

Which processes can you actually run? Stick, MIG, flux-core, TIG — and aluminium or stainless?

This decides what services we list, which is how Google decides what searches to show you for. Listing something you don't really do brings in the wrong calls and eventually a bad review.

Pipe fence and cattle guards — real, or something you'd grow into? Be honest here, it changes the plan

These are the searches worth the most money out in Cooke and Wise County, and far fewer people compete for them than for "welding." If you can do them well we aim at them hard.

What work do you want to turn down? Just as useful as what you want

Saying no to a job type sharpens everything. A site that's clearly for ranch and repair work beats a site that vaguely offers everything.

How many jobs a week can you handle? Realistically, not optimistically

There's no point sending you more calls than you can return. Unreturned calls turn into bad reviews, and bad reviews are the one thing that's genuinely hard to undo.

Your rate, minimum charge, and travel fee How far will you actually drive?

Putting a minimum on the site filters out the ten-dollar jobs before they reach your phone, and the drive radius tells us which towns to name on the site.

Group three

Your part, starting today

These two things will do more for your rankings than anything in the code. Neither one can be done for you.

Photograph every single job — before and after Phone camera is completely fine

This is the big one. A welding site with no pictures of welding doesn't get called, and Google ranks businesses with more photos higher in the map results. Start today, even on jobs that happen before launch.

Photos of you, the truck, and the rig A few good ones is plenty

For a one-man operation you are the trust signal. People hiring someone to come onto their property want to see who's turning up.

List ten past customers who'd leave you a review Let's start that list on this call

Reviews are the main thing separating the top three map results from everyone else. Your first ten to fifteen reviews are worth more than anything I can build. We'll set up a link you can text the day a job finishes.

Keep asking, every job, forever Ask in person, then send the link

Google weighs recent reviews more heavily than old ones, so this never really stops. A steady trickle beats a burst and then silence.

One real decision

The name is worth five minutes of thought

"Blue Star Welding Co." vs. something with Denton in it

Business names containing the search term — like "Denton Welding Co." — still measurably out-rank brand names for a search like welding denton tx. That's a real, if slightly annoying, fact about how Google works.

The trade-off: "Blue Star Welding Co." is the far better brand, it's what goes on the truck, and Google will suspend a profile whose name doesn't match your actual signage — so you can't just pick a keyword name on paper. This is your call, not mine. But it's free to decide now and expensive to change once you've filed the DBA and lettered the truck.
